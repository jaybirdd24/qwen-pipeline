On Saturday morning, Mina found a red lantern beside her grandmother's door. A folded note was tied to its handle with blue ribbon. The note invited her to bring the lantern to the hill before sunset. Mina packed a bottle of water, two apples, and a small blanket. Then she called her brother Leo, who was already waiting by the garden gate.

Their first stop was the bakery on the corner. The baker was lifting warm bread from a tray, and the whole shop smelled sweet. He gave the children a paper bag of little rolls for their journey. Mina tucked the bag carefully beside the apples. Leo thanked him and promised to tell him what they found on the hill.

Beyond the village, a wooden bridge crossed a narrow stream. The children stopped in the middle to watch silver fish moving below. One fish rested in the shadow of a stone while the others swam around it. Mina held the lantern close so its ribbon would not catch on the rail. On the far bank, they followed a path between tall grasses.

A sudden breeze carried a small hat across their path. Leo caught it before it reached the water. Its owner, an elderly gardener, came running from a nearby field. He laughed with relief and thanked Leo twice. Before returning to his beans, he pointed out a shorter path that passed an old pear tree and joined the road above the stream.

Under the pear tree, the children spread their blanket and shared the rolls. Mina counted three birds in the branches, but Leo spotted a fourth behind a leaf. They took turns describing its yellow feathers and tiny dark feet. After lunch, they gathered every crumb of paper, packed their bag, and checked that the lantern's handle was still secure.

The path grew steeper as they climbed. Leo slowed down near a bend, so Mina suggested counting their steps together. They counted twenty steps, rested beside a rock, and then counted twenty more. Soon the village roofs looked like little squares below them. The bakery chimney sent a thin line of smoke into the pale afternoon sky.

At the top of the hill, Grandmother was waiting beside a wooden bench. Several neighbours had brought lanterns of their own, in green, orange, and blue. She explained that the lights would welcome a family arriving in the village that evening. Mina placed her red lantern between two stones while Leo helped arrange the others along the safe edge of the path.

As the sun disappeared, the lanterns began to glow. Far below, a small group waved from the village road, and everyone on the hill waved back. Grandmother wrapped the blanket around the children while they watched the new family approach. Mina realised that the note had led them to more than a beautiful view. It had given them a way to say welcome.
